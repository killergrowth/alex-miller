If you own farm ground in Kansas, the question is simple: What is it worth today? The honest answer is that there is no single price per acre that applies across the state. A productive dryland quarter in north-central Kansas, an irrigated farm in the west, and a mixed pasture-and-hunting tract in southeast Kansas can trade at very different prices—even when they contain the same number of acres.

The best starting point is the latest statewide data. The better answer comes from narrowing that data to the county, land type, soil productivity, water, access, improvements, income potential, recreational appeal, and recent comparable sales surrounding the property.

As a Kansas auctioneer and real estate agent who works with farms, ranches, recreational tracts, and rural properties, I have learned that acreage alone rarely tells the full story. Buyers pay for what the land can produce, how easily it can be operated, what additional uses it offers, and how difficult it would be to find another tract like it.

## The Short Answer: 2026 Kansas Farmland Values

According to the USDA National Agricultural Statistics Service Land Values 2026 Summary, released July 31, 2026, Kansas farm real estate averaged $3,200 per acre. That broad measure includes the value of land and buildings on farms. The more useful land-type benchmarks are shown below.

| Kansas land category | 2026 average | 2025 average | Annual change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Farm real estate | $3,200/acre | $3,100/acre | +3.2% |
| All cropland | $3,540/acre | $3,440/acre | +2.9% |
| Irrigated cropland | $4,600/acre | $4,500/acre | +2.2% |
| Non-irrigated cropland | $3,450/acre | $3,350/acre | +3.0% |
| Pastureland | $2,400/acre | $2,270/acre | +5.7% |

*Source: USDA NASS, Land Values 2026 Summary, July 31, 2026. These are statewide averages, not appraisals of individual properties.*

These figures show that Kansas agricultural land values remained firm in 2026. Pasture recorded the strongest statewide percentage increase, while irrigated and non-irrigated cropland posted more modest gains. Still, a statewide average should be treated as a benchmark—not an asking price, reserve price, or appraisal.

## Why the Statewide Average Is Only a Starting Point

Kansas has 105 counties and several distinct agricultural regions. Rainfall, soils, crop mix, water availability, parcel size, recreation, and local buyer demand change considerably from one end of the state to the other. That is why two farms with similar acreage can sell for dramatically different amounts.

K-State's 2025 Kansas Agricultural Land Values and Trends report illustrates the spread. Its figures are based on open-market sales and should not be read as 2026 price guarantees, but they provide valuable regional context.

K-State reported 2025 non-irrigated averages ranging from $1,928 per acre in southwest Kansas to $7,363 in northeast Kansas. Pasture and hay averages ranged from $1,242 in west-central Kansas to $4,356 in northeast Kansas.

## The 10 Factors That Most Affect Kansas Farm Ground Value

### 1. Location and Local Demand

Location influences who will compete for the property. A farm next to an expanding operator, close to grain handling, near a paved road, or within reach of a growing community may attract a deeper buyer pool.

### 2. Soil Type and Productivity

Productive soils generally support stronger yields, rent, and resale demand. Buyers often review soil maps, capability classes, slope, drainage, and a productivity measure such as NCCPI.

### 3. Tillable Acres and Field Efficiency

Gross acres and productive acres are not always the same. Buyers want to know how many acres are actually tillable, how the fields lay, and whether large equipment can move efficiently.

### 4. Water and Irrigation

In western and south-central Kansas, irrigation can materially affect value, but the word "irrigated" is not enough. A proper analysis considers the water right, authorized quantity, historic use, well capacity, equipment condition, energy source, and any local groundwater restrictions.

### 5. Farm Income and Lease Terms

Land value is closely tied to its ability to generate income. Buyers may examine cash rent, crop-share terms, pasture rent, government program payments, hunting income, mineral income, and operating expenses.

### 6. Access, Roads, and Easements

Good legal access is essential. Paved or well-maintained gravel frontage can expand the buyer pool, while landlocked property, poor roads, shared access, or unclear easements can reduce value.

### 7. Fencing, Water, and Improvements

For pasture and ranch ground, fence condition, cross-fencing, ponds, wells, tanks, corrals, and working facilities have real economic value.

### 8. Recreation and Wildlife Habitat

Kansas land is not purchased only for agricultural return. Timber, creek or river frontage, ponds, wetlands, grass cover, food plots, travel corridors, and proven deer, turkey, or waterfowl habitat can bring recreational buyers into the market.

### 9. Tract Size, Shape, and Divisibility

A smaller tract may sell for more per acre because more buyers can afford the total purchase. A large, contiguous block can command a premium from serious operators or investors, but the higher total price may narrow the pool.

### 10. Sale Method and Market Exposure

Value is not determined only by the property. It is also influenced by how the property is presented and sold. An auction can be effective when a property has several likely buyers and the seller wants competition, a definite timeline, and transparent price discovery.

## What the 2026 Kansas Land Market Is Telling Us

The 2026 market is best described as firm but selective. USDA's statewide estimates increased across the major Kansas categories from 2025 to 2026. For sellers, this means quality and presentation matter. Strong soils, efficient fields, reliable water, usable improvements, good access, clean documentation, and recreational features can separate one tract from the general market.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Kansas Farmland Prices

**What is the average price of Kansas farm ground in 2026?**
USDA reports a 2026 Kansas average of $3,540 per acre for all cropland, including $4,600 for irrigated cropland and $3,450 for non-irrigated cropland. Pasture averages $2,400 per acre. Individual sales can fall well above or below these benchmarks.

**Is irrigated land always worth more than dryland?**
Irrigated land generally sells for more when it has a dependable water right, productive well, serviceable equipment, and favorable operating economics. Weak water, restrictions, high energy costs, or worn-out equipment can reduce the premium.

**Does hunting ground sell for more than pasture?**
It can. High-quality habitat, water, timber, access, privacy, and a strong local reputation can attract recreational buyers who value the tract beyond its grazing income.

**Does a county tax appraisal show what my farm will sell for?**
No. Kansas agricultural land is valued for property-tax purposes based on agricultural use and productivity rather than open-market sale value. A market analysis should rely on comparable sales, income, land quality, and current demand.
